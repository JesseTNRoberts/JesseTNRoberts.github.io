An advanced, practical course in deep learning that pairs the mathematical foundations with the
skills to design, implement, train, evaluate, and deploy deep neural networks: MLPs, residual CNNs,
RNNs, LSTMs, GRUs, transformers, and more. Coursework is hands-on coding on real-world datasets,
using high-performance computing resources. Prerequisite: [CSC 4220, Introduction to Machine
Learning]({{ '/teaching/SP2025-CSC4220' | relative_url }}).

**Students will learn to:**

- derive and implement backpropagation, and use automatic differentiation and gradient checking;
- understand what makes deep networks trainable: initialization, activation functions, residual
  connections, and batch and layer normalization;
- choose, build, and train the right architecture for a problem (MLPs, CNNs, RNN/LSTM/GRU, transformers);
- apply regularization, optimizers like momentum and Adam, learning-rate schedules, and hyperparameter tuning;
- use transfer learning, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, and data augmentation;
- build reproducible training pipelines with checkpointing and experiment tracking;
- recognize ethical, safety, and fairness issues in deep learning systems.

**Topics.** Foundations review · PyTorch · variance and gradient flow · vanishing and exploding
gradients · residual connections and normalization · learning-rate scheduling, Adam, and
regularization · CNNs, RNNs, and transformers · self-supervised learning, foundation models, and
parameter-efficient fine-tuning · representation learning · responsible AI.

**Format.** Lectures, discussion, and in-class coding; assignments are designed to be done in class.
AI assistance isn't allowed unless an assignment says so, since the goal is being able to build
these methods yourself. **Grading:** assignments 30% · four quizzes 20% · two exams 50%. Students
in AI 5200 do extended assignments plus a long-form book reading and discussion.
