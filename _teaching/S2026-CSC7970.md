This is a seminar on reading and discussing the papers that introduced and analyzed the important
LLMs. In the first half, everyone reads the same papers each week, writes a response, and finds a
follow-up paper of their own that pushes the idea toward their research area. In the second half,
students propose, carry out, and write up an original research project that studies or applies LLMs.
The goal is research initiation: each student leaves with a paper-shaped project in their own field.

**Students will:**

- understand the transformer architecture and its encoder-only and decoder-only variants;
- find papers that extend the well-known results toward their own research area;
- propose and carry out an original publication about LLMs.

**Format.** Weekly synchronous discussion (Fridays), led by students, with 5–8 hours a week of
independent reading. Generative AI is part of the course: students use LLMs throughout, though paper
responses must be their own writing.

**Grading.** Paper responses 40% · discussion participation and leadership 20% · conference-style
paper presentation 5% · research project 35%.

The papers below are the core of my [LLM foundations reading list]({{ page.reading_list }}), which is
a good place to start if you're learning how modern language models came to be.
