A survey of how computer systems are designed: instruction set architecture, microarchitecture,
digital logic, pipelined processor design, the memory hierarchy, and parallel processing.

**Students will learn to:**

- analyze how design decisions affect the performance of different processor designs;
- relate microarchitectures to the instruction set they implement;
- design digital circuits;
- understand instruction-level and processor-level parallelism.

**Texts.** *Computer Organization and Design, RISC-V Edition: The Hardware/Software Interface*
(2nd ed.) by Patterson and Hennessy, with Robert Winkler's *RISC-V Assembly Programming*.

**Format.** Lectures, discussion and in-class work (all graded work happens in class).
**Grading:** assignments 35% · five quizzes 25% · two exams 40%.
